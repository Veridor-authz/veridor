# Code of conduct

## The standard

Be respectful. Assume competence and good faith. Disagree about the work, not
about the person doing it.

This project is about refusing to claim more than was measured. That discipline
applies to conversation too: say what you observed, say what you inferred, and
keep the two apart. "This is wrong" needs a reproduction; "I think this is
wrong" does not, and the difference matters.

## Not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning comments, unwelcome attention, publishing
others' private information, or sustained disruption of discussion.

## Security reports are different

Do not open a public issue for a vulnerability. See
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md): private reporting exists precisely so a reporter is
never forced to choose between disclosure and being ignored.

## Enforcement

Report through the private vulnerability reporting form linked from
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) and say it is a conduct report; it is not public.
If the form is unavailable, write to opensource@veridor.org and say it is a
conduct report. If that fails too, open a public issue with the "Question" issue
form asking for a contact, and leave out every detail of the report.

Veridor has one maintainer, @Santoshkumarpuppala
([GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)), who reads those reports and keeps them
confidential. The maintainer may edit, remove, or reject contributions and
comments that violate this document, and may ban an account for sustained or
severe violations.

The maintainer is subject to this document too. With one maintainer, a report
about the maintainer cannot be handed to someone else inside the project; that
changes when a second maintainer joins.

## Scope

Applies in project spaces (repositories, issues and pull requests) and when
representing the project publicly.
